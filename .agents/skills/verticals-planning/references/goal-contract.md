# Goal contract

## Causal-link test

For every `parent -> child` edge, require all five answers:

1. Is the parent accepted or directly confirmed?
2. Is the child supported by a direct instruction, agreement, or promise?
3. Does completing the child materially advance the parent?
4. Can the relationship be explained without inventing a strategy?
5. Is this the strongest primary parent?

Any “no” blocks the mutation.

## Card contract

Prefer short, human outcome titles. Keep one testable thought per card. State numbers precisely: amount, period, scope, and whether the target is a one-time crossing or a sustained level.

Every active card needs a concise note aligned with verified sources. Higher verticals explain identity, direction, or the chosen bet. Lower verticals explain route, result, measurement, or observable completion.

Do not repeat information already visible in the interface: title, parent chain, vertical, date, owner, tags, IDs, colors, or child list. Keep source bookkeeping and internal reasoning out of the body. Use a document-style body only when the card is intentionally storing substantive documentation.

Before proposing a card, verify:

- exact source and date exist;
- owner is explicit;
- title is an outcome or direct action, not a vague theme;
- acceptance criterion is observable;
- parent is causal;
- body adds useful context at the correct abstraction level;
- date follows explicit timing or a capacity basis;
- missing information remains an unresolved choice, not invented prose.

## Capacity contract

Dates are commitments. For substantial work, inventory fixed obligations, usable capacity, coordination overhead, recovery, context switching, and comparable completed work. If scope, deadline, and capacity conflict, present explicit trade-offs. Do not use an empty-looking calendar as proof of capacity.

For small incoming tasks, the user's explicit timing controls. If timing is unclear, ask whether it belongs today, tomorrow, this week, or the unscheduled backlog.

## Write protocol

1. Read the current state.
2. Draft the exact hierarchy and mutation diff.
3. Obtain approval.
4. Apply only approved content and evidence writes.
5. Reread changed goals, descendants, and board slices.
6. Report verified state and any discrepancy.
